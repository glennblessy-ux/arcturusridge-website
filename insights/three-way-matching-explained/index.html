3-way matching explained: how it stops duplicate and fraudulent payments

What 3-way matching is, how it differs from 2-way matching, the exceptions it catches and how to run it without slowing your accounts payable team down.

ArcturusRidge23 November 20266 min read

Most overpayments are not dramatic frauds. They are ordinary mistakes: an invoice paid twice, a price that crept up, goods billed before they arrived. 3-way matching is the control that catches them before the money leaves your account.

What is 3-way matching?

3-way matching is a check that an accounts payable team runs before approving a supplier invoice for payment. It compares three documents:

  1. The purchase order (PO) — what your company agreed to buy, at what price and in what quantity.
  2. The goods receipt note (GRN) — what was actually delivered and accepted by your warehouse or site team.
  3. The supplier invoice — what the supplier is asking you to pay.

If all three agree, the invoice is cleared for payment. If they don't, the invoice is held as an exception until someone resolves the difference.

2-way vs 3-way vs 4-way matching

TypeDocuments comparedTypically used for
2-wayPO and invoiceServices and low-risk purchases where there is no physical delivery to check
3-wayPO, goods receipt and invoicePhysical goods, raw materials, inventory and most stock purchases
4-wayPO, goods receipt, quality inspection and invoiceRegulated or quality-critical items such as pharma inputs and engineered components

The right level depends on risk. Applying 4-way matching to stationery wastes time; applying 2-way matching to raw materials leaves you paying for goods that may never have arrived.

What 3-way matching catches

  • Quantity differences — billed for 1,000 units when 950 were received.
  • Price differences — an invoice at ₹128 per unit against a PO rate of ₹120.
  • Duplicate invoices — the same invoice submitted twice, often with a slightly different invoice number or by email and post.
  • Invoices without a valid PO — purchases that bypassed your approval process.
  • Goods not yet received — invoices raised before delivery, which should wait until the GRN is posted.
  • Tax mismatches — the wrong GST rate, a missing GSTIN, or a supplier GSTIN that doesn't match your vendor master.

Tolerances: the setting that makes or breaks the process

Strict matching on every rupee creates a flood of exceptions for trivial rounding differences. Most companies set tolerances — for example, allowing a price difference of up to 1% or ₹500, whichever is lower, and no tolerance on quantity. Anything inside the tolerance clears automatically; anything outside goes to an exception queue.

Review tolerances every quarter. If one supplier constantly sits just inside the limit, that is worth a conversation.

How exceptions should be handled

An exception is only useful if it gets resolved quickly. A good exception workflow:

  1. Routes the invoice to the person who can fix it — the buyer for price issues, the stores team for quantity issues.
  2. Contacts the supplier directly for credit notes or corrected invoices.
  3. Records the reason for every exception, so recurring problems with a supplier or a site are visible.
  4. Never lets an unresolved exception slip into a payment run.

Common reasons 3-way matching fails in practice

  • GRNs posted late. If the warehouse records receipts days after delivery, invoices sit in exceptions for no reason.
  • POs raised after the invoice. "Retro POs" defeat the purpose of the control.
  • Messy vendor master data. Duplicate vendor records make duplicate payments much harder to spot.
  • Manual matching on spreadsheets. It works at 200 invoices a month. At 2,000 it becomes the bottleneck.

Doing it at scale

Modern AP teams combine automated data capture with people who understand the exceptions. Software reads the invoice and runs the match; trained specialists deal with the 10–20% of invoices that don't match cleanly, chase suppliers and keep the vendor master clean. That combination is what keeps duplicate payments out without slowing down month-end close.

In-house vs outsourced accounts payable: what does it really cost?

How to work out the true cost of running accounts payable in-house, the hidden costs most companies miss, and when outsourcing to a managed AP team makes sense.

ArcturusRidge23 November 20265 min read

Ask most finance teams what accounts payable costs and they'll quote the salaries of the people who process invoices. That number is real, but it's rarely the whole picture.

The visible cost: people

Start with the fully loaded cost of everyone who touches invoices — not just AP clerks, but the share of a finance manager's time spent on approvals, vendor queries and month-end. "Fully loaded" means salary plus:

  • benefits, bonuses and statutory contributions;
  • office space, equipment and software licences;
  • recruitment and training, including the time it takes a new hire to become productive.

The hidden costs

These rarely appear in a budget line, but they are often larger than the people cost:

  • Duplicate and incorrect payments. Without consistent matching and duplicate checks, overpayments slip through and are hard to recover.
  • Missed early-payment discounts. Slow approvals mean losing discounts that suppliers would happily give.
  • Late-payment charges and supplier friction. Late payments damage relationships and can cost you priority, credit terms or better prices.
  • Lost tax credits. In India, poor GST reconciliation and invoices that age beyond 180 days unpaid can cost input tax credit.
  • Attrition and cover. When an AP specialist resigns or goes on leave, invoices pile up — usually just before month-end.
  • Management time. Every hour your finance leaders spend chasing approvals and answering vendor emails is an hour not spent on planning and analysis.

A simple way to estimate your number

  1. List everyone who works on AP and the percentage of their time it takes.
  2. Multiply each person's fully loaded monthly cost by that percentage, and add them up.
  3. Add your monthly AP software and tool costs.
  4. Estimate the hidden costs above, even roughly — for example, discounts missed last quarter.
  5. Divide the total by the number of invoices you process each month to get a cost per invoice.

What outsourcing changes

A managed AP service replaces fixed headcount with a dedicated team that works inside your existing ERP and follows your approval rules. The model typically changes the economics in three ways:

  • Lower cost per specialist through an offshore delivery team and shared supervision.
  • Predictable monthly spend — a flat rate per full-time equivalent (FTE) rather than unpredictable hiring, overtime and temporary staff.
  • Built-in continuity — leave and resignations are the provider's problem, not yours.

What you should keep in-house

Outsourcing the processing is not the same as outsourcing control. Keep these with your own team:

  • final approval and release of payments from your bank portal;
  • approval limits and policy;
  • supplier strategy and commercial negotiations.

When outsourcing makes sense

  • You process more than a few hundred invoices a month and volume is growing.
  • Month-end close is regularly delayed by AP.
  • You depend on one or two people who hold the process in their heads.
  • You have had duplicate payments, lost discounts or GST credit issues in the past year.
  • You want finance staff working on analysis rather than data entry.

Questions to ask any AP provider

  • Will our specialists work only on our account?
  • Can you work inside our ERP, or do we need new software?
  • How do you protect our data, and who can access what?
  • What are your SLAs for processing time and accuracy, and how are they reported?
  • Who keeps final control over payments?

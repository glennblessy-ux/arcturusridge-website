GST compliance for accounts payable teams: protecting your input tax credit

A practical checklist for Indian accounts payable teams: checking supplier invoices, reconciling with GSTR-2B, the 180-day payment rule and the deadlines that decide whether you keep your input tax credit.

ArcturusRidge23 November 20267 min read

For an Indian business, accounts payable isn't only about paying suppliers on time. Every purchase invoice carries input tax credit (ITC), and small AP mistakes — a wrong GSTIN, a missed reconciliation, a late payment — can quietly turn that credit into a cost.

This article is general information, not tax advice. GST rules and portal processes change; confirm the current position with your tax adviser.

Why AP owns so much of GST compliance

Input tax credit can only be claimed when certain conditions are met, and most of them are checked — or missed — in accounts payable. Broadly, you need to:

  • hold a valid tax invoice or debit note from a registered supplier;
  • have actually received the goods or services;
  • see the invoice reflected in your auto-generated statement (GSTR-2B) after the supplier files their return;
  • be able to show that the supplier has paid the tax to the government;
  • pay the supplier within the time the law allows.

1. Check every invoice before it is booked

Build these checks into invoice processing, not into month-end:

  • GSTINs — the supplier's GSTIN is valid and active, and your own GSTIN is correct for the receiving location.
  • Place of supply — this decides whether IGST or CGST + SGST applies. A wrong split is one of the most common errors.
  • HSN/SAC code and rate — the tax rate matches what you agreed and what applies to the item.
  • Mandatory fields — invoice number, date, taxable value and tax amounts.
  • E-invoice details — where the supplier is required to issue e-invoices, check for a valid IRN and QR code. An invoice that should have been e-invoiced but wasn't is a risk to your credit.

2. Reconcile your books with GSTR-2B every month

GSTR-2B is a monthly statement on the GST portal showing the invoices your suppliers have reported against your GSTIN. It is the primary basis for the ITC you can claim. Reconciling it against your purchase register each month shows:

  • invoices in your books but missing from GSTR-2B — the supplier hasn't filed or has reported them incorrectly;
  • invoices in GSTR-2B but not in your books — possibly an unrecorded purchase, or an invoice you never received;
  • value or tax differences between the two.

The GST portal's Invoice Management System also lets you accept, reject or keep pending the invoices suppliers report, before your GSTR-2B is finalised. Decide who in your team reviews these each month and what the rules are for rejecting an invoice.

Follow up missing invoices with suppliers immediately. The longer you wait, the harder it is to recover the credit.

3. Watch the 180-day payment rule

If you claim ITC on an invoice but don't pay the supplier — value plus tax — within 180 days of the invoice date, the credit has to be reversed, with interest. You can re-claim it once you pay. For AP, that means:

  • track the age of every unpaid invoice on which credit has been claimed;
  • flag invoices approaching 180 days before they cross it;
  • pay attention to disputed and on-hold invoices, which are the ones most likely to age out.

4. Know the ITC deadline for each financial year

ITC for a financial year must be claimed by the earlier of 30 November of the following year or the date you file the annual return. Invoices found late — in a drawer, an old mailbox or a supplier's statement — can lose their credit permanently. A monthly reconciliation is the best protection.

5. Don't forget TDS and MSME payment timelines

Two other checks usually sit with AP:

  • TDS under the Income-tax law — deduct tax at the right rate on contractor, professional and other specified payments, deposit it on time and issue certificates. Keep vendor PANs and lower-deduction certificates on file.
  • Payments to micro and small enterprises — the MSMED Act sets a maximum payment period of 45 days where there is a written agreement (15 days without one), and income-tax rules can disallow the expense if payment is late. Tag MSME suppliers in your vendor master so their invoices are prioritised.

The bottom line

Most lost input tax credit comes from routine process gaps, not complex tax questions. A disciplined AP process — validation at entry, monthly GSTR-2B reconciliation and ageing controls — protects credit that would otherwise be written off.
